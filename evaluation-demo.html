Why is the support bot failing customers?

An agent that scores every customer conversation against a quality rubric, groups conversations into themes, finds what broke between two bot versions, and proposes a fix for a human to approve.

The agent’s investigation

The agent plans its own steps and calls tools. It stops before changing anything and asks a human to approve the fix.

    Finding

    Evidence:

    Policy 3.1 Returns are accepted within 30 days. Exceptions require a human agent.
    Policy 3.2 Clearance items are final sale.
    Policy 3.3 Opened bedding and mattresses can’t be returned.
    Policy 3.6 Refunds go to the original payment method.

    Proposed fix

    1. Replace “Always make it right for the customer” with “Offer only actions the return policy allows.”
    2. Retrieve the return-eligibility rules (policy 3.1–3.6) before any refund, exception, or payment change.
    3. Hand final-sale, late, and opened-item requests to a human agent.
    4. Add the refund conversations to the regression test set and re-run before release.

    Expected outcome: policy safety returns to v1 levels while keeping v2’s gains in resolution and tone.

    Waiting for human approval.

    Method

    The rubric

    A conversation passes overall when it’s resolved and no dimension fails.

    DimensionPassPartialFail
    ResolvedIssue fully fixed in the chatValid next step offered, not yet doneNot fixed or wrong path
    AccurateAll facts match policy and product dataMinor detail offWrong fact the customer could act on
    Policy safeOnly offers allowed actionsVague or borderline promisePromises an action policy doesn’t allow
    ToneClear, warm, conciseRobotic or wordyRude or dismissive

    Models

    RoleModelWhy
    Support botClaude Haiku 5.5Fast and cheap, like many production bots
    Judge and agentClaude Sonnet 5.5Reasoning quality for scoring and investigation
    Spot checkClaude Opus 5.5Re-grades disputed cases only

    The judge is a different, stronger model than the bot, which avoids a model grading its own work too generously.

    Real: the rubric, the evaluation design, the agent workflow, and every number on this site, which is calculated from the conversations shown.
